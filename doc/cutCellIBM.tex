\title{\bf A cut-cell immersed boundary method for static and moving bodies\\
with passive scalar transport in a collocated finite-volume solver\\[3pt]
\large Formulation, implementation in OpenFOAM and verification}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We present a cut-cell immersed boundary method for the incompressible Navier--Stokes
equations on a fixed, non body-fitted mesh%
, its extension to rigid bodies moving through the mesh%
, and the transport of passive scalars in the same framework%
. The body is described by a level set; each face of the mesh carries the fraction of its
area that lies in the fluid and each cell the fraction of its volume. The wall segment of a
cut cell is not computed independently but \emph{defined} by the geometric closure of the
fluid polyhedron, and this single identity carries the whole boundary treatment: the wall
pressure force is absorbed into a face sum, no-penetration follows from discrete continuity,
and the difference between a free-slip and a no-slip wall is one diagonal coefficient. No
ghost values, no extrapolation into the solid and no coupling between velocity components
appear.
For a moving body only continuity changes: it acquires the flux swept by the wall as a
source, and taking that flux --- rather than the geometric volume change --- as the primary
quantity makes the discrete geometric conservation law hold by construction, so that a free
stream is preserved to machine precision at any body speed.
Passive scalars are transported by the same face fluxes and see the wall either as a
fixed-value or as a zero-flux surface; their budgets close to round-off.
An optional second-order wall treatment replaces the cell centres by the centroids of the
fluid parts, with a centroid-consistent diffusive face flux and a centroid wall distance; it
raises the operators and the velocity from first to second order without ghost values or
stencil growth.
The method is implemented in OpenFOAM without modifying the mesh: the fractions enter the
standard operators explicitly, which keeps the pressure--velocity coupling of
\code{simpleFoam}/\code{pimpleFoam} and the linear algebra untouched%
, and the whole moving-body extension sits behind a single run-time switch%
. The implementation is verified against an independent reference implementation of the
same discretisation on a cylinder at $Re_D=1$%
\ at rest, oscillating and translating through a periodic seam%
, by conservation tests for the scalar%
, and by order-of-accuracy studies on manufactured and exact solutions%
.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Immersed boundary methods let a body of arbitrary shape be represented on a mesh that does
not conform to it. Among them, cut-cell methods are distinguished by being conservative:
the fluid part of every cell is a well-defined control volume, and fluxes are accounted for on
its actual boundary. Their classical difficulties are the description of the cut geometry,
the small-cell problem%
, and, for moving bodies, the consistency between the change of the fluid
volume of a cell and the flux swept by its wall, i.e.\ the discrete geometric
conservation law%
.

The method described here addresses these points with a minimum of machinery. Its central
object is the \emph{closure identity} of a cut cell: the wet parts of its faces and its wall
segment form a closed surface, so the wall area vector equals minus the sum of the wet face
area vectors. Rather than computing the wall segment from the geometry and then enforcing
consistency, the method defines the wall segment through the identity. Every consequence of
the boundary condition --- pressure force, no-penetration, wall traction%
, and for a moving body the volume swept%
\ --- is then expressed with the same face fractions that the flux operators use, and the
resulting discrete properties (exactness for uniform flow, no spurious force from a uniform
pressure, exact compatibility of the pressure system%
, free-stream preservation%
) hold to round-off by construction.

\section{Governing equations}
\label{sec:equations}

We solve the incompressible Navier--Stokes equations
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \uu}{\partial t}+\nabla\!\cdot\!(\uu\uu)-\nabla\!\cdot\!(\nu\nabla\uu)
=-\nabla p+\bm{g},
\qquad \nabla\!\cdot\!\uu=0,
\label{eq:ns}
\end{equation}
with $p$ the kinematic pressure, $\nu$ the kinematic viscosity and $\bm{g}$ a body force, in
a domain $\Omega$ containing one or more rigid bodies $B_i$%
\ with prescribed velocity $\ub(\xx,t)$: zero for a body at rest,
$\ub=\bm U(t)+\bm\omega\times(\xx-\bm c(t))$ for a rigid body with centre $\bm c(t)$. On each
body surface one of two conditions is imposed: \emph{no slip}, $\uu=\ub$, or \emph{free slip},
$(\uu-\ub)\cdot\nn=0$ together with zero tangential traction,
$(\bm I-\nn\nn)\cdot(\nabla\uu+\nabla\uu^{\!\top})\cdot\nn=\bm 0$.
Passive scalars $T_k$, $k=1,\dots,K$, obey
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}+\nabla\!\cdot\!(\uu T)-\nabla\!\cdot\!(D\nabla T)=q,
\label{eq:scalar}
\end{equation}
each with its own constant diffusivity $D$, volumetric source $q$ and, on each body, either a
fixed value $T=T_w$ or zero diffusive flux $\partial T/\partial n=0$. The coupling is one-way:
the flow does not depend on the scalars.

The discretisation is a collocated finite-volume method with SIMPLE(C) pressure--velocity
coupling in the arrangement of OpenFOAM's \code{simpleFoam} (steady) and \code{pimpleFoam}
(transient, implicit Euler with outer iterations). We write $V_P$ for the volume of cell $P$,
$\Sf$ for the area vector of face $f$ pointing out of $P$, $N$ for the neighbour across $f$,
$\phi_f$ for the volume flux through $f$, $\bm d_f=\xx_N-\xx_P$ and $\delta_f=1/|\bm d_f|$ for
the inverse of the centre-to-centre distance. Face values $(\cdot)_f$ are linear interpolates,
$\tfrac12[(\cdot)_P+(\cdot)_N]$ on a uniform mesh. Sums $\sum_f$ run over the faces of the
cell under consideration.
\section{Geometric description of the body}
\label{sec:geom}

\subsection{Level set, fluid fractions and cell classes}

\begin{definition}[Level set and fractions]
A body is given by a function $\varphi(\xx)$, negative inside the body and positive in the
fluid; only its sign and its zero level are used, and $\varphi=0$ counts as solid. For a face
$f$ and a cell $P$,
\begin{equation}
\theta_f=\frac{|\{\xx\in f:\varphi>0\}|}{|\Sf|}\in[0,1],\qquad
\alpha_P=\frac{|\{\xx\in P:\varphi>0\}|}{V_P}\in[0,1]
\label{eq:fractions}
\end{equation}
are the fluid area and volume fractions. Several bodies are combined through the pointwise
minimum of their level sets, which represents their union exactly.
\end{definition}

Cells fall into three classes (Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}a): \emph{fluid} ($\alpha_P=1$),
\emph{solid} ($\alpha_P=0$) and \emph{cut} ($0<\alpha_P<1$). A fourth set, overlapping the
others, collects the \emph{wall cells}: every cell with $\alpha_P>0$ whose wall area
(defined below) exceeds $10^{-10}V_P^{2/3}$. It contains all cut cells and, in addition, the
fluid cells one of whose faces has been closed by a solid neighbour (Section~\ref{sec:absorb}).
Wall cells are the only cells in which a wall term appears.

The fluid part $\Omega_P$ of a cut cell is a polyhedron bounded by the wet parts of the cell
faces, of area vectors $\theta_f\Sf$, and by the wall segment, of area $\Aw$ and outward unit
normal $\nn_w$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}b). Its volume is $\alpha_PV_P$.

\subsection{The closure identity}

\begin{proposition}[Closure]
\label{prop:closure}
For every cell, $\sum_f\theta_f\Sf+\nn_w\Aw=\bm 0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the divergence theorem to a constant vector field $\bm a$ over $\Omega_P$:
$0=\int_{\Omega_P}\nabla\!\cdot\!\bm a\dd V=\bm a\cdot\big(\sum_f\theta_f\Sf+\nn_w\Aw\big)$
for every $\bm a$. For a planar wall segment $\nn_w\Aw$ is its area vector; for a curved one
it is the integral $\int_{\rm wall}\nn\dd S$, and the identity holds unchanged.
\end{proof}

We write
\begin{equation}
\Sw\equiv-\sum_f\theta_f\Sf=\nn_w\Aw,\qquad \Aw=|\Sw|,
\label{eq:Sw}
\end{equation}
for the wall area vector, pointing from the fluid into the body. In the implementation $\Sw$
is never computed from the level set: it is \emph{defined} by \eqref{eq:Sw} from the same
$\theta_f$ that weight every flux operator, so the identity holds to round-off however the wall
is inclined or curved%
\ and, for a moving body, however fast it moves%
. Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}c shows the construction. Two consequences are used throughout.
First, every property that follows from the divergence theorem for a closed surface holds
discretely: a constant field has zero net flux, a uniform pressure exerts no force. Second,
the geometric errors of the fractions (the chord instead of the arc on a face, the
quadrature error of $\alpha_P$) change \emph{where} the discrete wall is, but never make the
discrete control volume leak.

In a cell whose faces are all fully open or fully closed, \eqref{eq:Sw} also gives a
non-zero $\Sw$ as soon as one face is closed: for a fluid cell next to a solid neighbour,
$\Sw$ is the area vector of the closed face, i.e.\ the cell has a staircase wall. The two
cases need no distinction in any operator.

\subsection{Computation of the fractions}
\label{sec:geomcomp}

The fractions are computed once for bodies at rest and at every time step when a body moves,
as follows.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
\item \emph{Evaluation.} The union level set is evaluated at the mesh points, the face
      centres and the cell centres.
\item \emph{Edge crossings.} On every edge whose end points have different fluid status,
      the crossing is located by bisection of the exact level set, $n_{\rm bis}=40$ steps by
      default. The edge is always traversed in a canonical direction, from the
      lexicographically smaller end point $A$ to the larger one $B$: with bracket
      $[\lambda_{\rm lo},\lambda_{\rm hi}]\subset[0,1]$, the midpoint
      $\xx_m=A+\tfrac12(\lambda_{\rm lo}+\lambda_{\rm hi})(B-A)$ replaces the end of the
      bracket that has the same fluid status, and the crossing is the final midpoint. Its
      error is at most $2^{-41}\approx4.5\times10^{-13}$ of the edge length. The canonical
      direction makes the two copies of an edge on a processor boundary, which have identical
      coordinates, obtain bit-identical crossings; across a translational periodic boundary
      the lexicographic order is preserved, so the two sides agree whenever the level set
      is itself periodic. All cut edges are bisected together, one level-set call per
      iteration, which matters for bodies whose level set is expensive (triangulated
      surfaces).
\item \emph{Face fractions.} A face whose vertices are all fluid has $\theta_f=1$, one whose
      vertices are all solid $\theta_f=0$. Otherwise the vertices are walked in order;
      each fluid vertex is appended to a polygon, and after every vertex whose status differs
      from the next the crossing of that edge is appended. The polygon area
      $\big|\sum_i\tfrac12(\bm p_i-\bm p_0)\times(\bm p_{i+1}-\bm p_0)\big|$ divided by
      $|\Sf|$ is $\theta_f$. A face with alternating signs (a saddle) is resolved as
      \emph{fluid-connected}: the walk yields one polygon joining both fluid corners. The
      wet part of the face is thus approximated by the polygon whose edges are chords of the
      wall's trace on the face, a second-order approximation in the mesh spacing.
      The centroid $\xx'_f$ of the polygon, by fan triangulation with the triangles weighted
      by their area projected on the face normal, and the fraction are kept as computed,
      before absorption and synchronisation (the geometric cut, $\theta^{\rm geo}_f$), for the
      rotating-wall source of Section~\ref{sec:ubpoint}%
      \ and the wall pieces of Section~\ref{sec:grad2}%
      .
\item \emph{Cell fractions.} A cell is a candidate for sub-sampling when the level set
      at its centre, its face centres and its vertices does not have one sign; the others
      have $\alpha_P=1$ or $0$. For a candidate, $\alpha_P$ is the fraction of fluid points
      in a lattice of $n_s$ points per direction ($n_s=32$ by default) placed at the
      sub-cell centres of the cell's bounding box,
      \begin{equation}
      \alpha_P=\frac{\#\{k:\varphi(\xx_k)>0\}}{\#\{k:\xx_k\in P\}},
      \label{eq:subsample}
      \end{equation}
      where samples outside the cell are discarded (the point-in-cell test is skipped for
      axis-aligned hexahedra, whose bounding box is the cell). In a direction that is not
      solved (the empty direction of a two-dimensional case) a single sample at the cell
      centre is taken, so a two-dimensional case samples $n_s^2$ points and a
      three-dimensional one $n_s^3$. This midpoint quadrature has an error of order
      $1/n_s$ per cell for a generic cut (about $10^{-3}$ for $n_s=32$), which is small
      compared with the discretisation error%
      \ but, as Section~\ref{sec:primary} shows, not small enough to be differentiated in time%
      .
\item \emph{Sliver absorption and coupled faces}, Section~\ref{sec:absorb}.
\item \emph{Derived quantities}, Section~\ref{sec:derived}.
\end{enumerate}
Two features smaller than a cell are not resolved: a body that crosses a face without
crossing any of its edges leaves $\theta_f$ unchanged, and one that enters a cell without
reaching its centre, a face centre or a vertex leaves $\alpha_P=1$. Note also that $\alpha_P$
and $\theta_f$ come from different algorithms and are therefore not exactly consistent (the
polyhedron implied by the $\theta_f$ does not have exactly the volume $\alpha_PV_P$); nothing
in the method requires them to be, because $\alpha_P$ enters only as a volume weight and
$\Sw$ is built from $\theta_f$ alone.

\subsection{Sliver absorption and coupled faces}
\label{sec:absorb}

A cut cell with a tiny fluid volume but open faces is the source of the small-cell problem.
The fractions are therefore post-processed in two steps (Fig.~\ref{fig:sliver}).

\emph{Absorption.} Every cell with $\alpha_P<\alpha_{\min}$ ($\alpha_{\min}=0.02$ by default)
is absorbed into the body: $\alpha_P:=0$ and \emph{all its faces are closed}, $\theta_f:=0$.
The same rule closes the faces of every cell that has $\alpha_P=0$ from the start, so a solid
cell never has an open face. Closing the faces is essential: an open face between an absorbed
cell and a fluid neighbour would let the neighbour exchange flux with a cell that carries no
equation, and the closure identity would then place the neighbour's wall on a face that is not
a wall. After absorption the neighbours see the closed face through \eqref{eq:Sw} as part of
their wall (Fig.~\ref{fig:sliver}b), so the treatment is automatically consistent. The
absorption displaces the boundary by at most $O(\alpha_{\min}h)$ and removes the fluid volume
$\alpha_PV_P<\alpha_{\min}V_P$ of each absorbed cell. The pass is not iterated: a neighbour
that loses faces keeps its $\alpha$.

\emph{Coupled faces.} The two copies of a face on a periodic or processor boundary must carry
the same fraction. After absorption, $\theta_f$ is synchronised across coupled faces with the
minimum of the two copies,
\begin{equation}
\theta_f:=\min(\theta_f,\theta_{f'}),
\end{equation}
which also propagates the closing of an absorbed cell across the boundary. The order matters
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sliver}c): synchronising first and absorbing second would close the face on the
side of the absorbed cell only, and its partner cell would keep an open face into a cell
without equation.
Section~\ref{sec:galilean} shows the effect on a body crossing a periodic seam.
A difference larger than $10^{-10}$ between the two copies before synchronisation is reported
as a warning (a body that is not periodic although the mesh is).

spacing $0.2$. Closed faces are black lines; red arrows are the wall vectors $\Sw$ of the

$\alpha_P=0.018$. (b)~After absorption its faces are closed, and the neighbour above it

\subsection{Derived quantities}
\label{sec:derived}

From the fractions the following fields are formed.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep3pt
\item The wall vector $\Sw$ by \eqref{eq:Sw}, accumulated face by face: each internal face
      subtracts $\theta_f\Sf$ from its owner and adds it to its neighbour, each boundary face
      subtracts it from its cell. Boundary faces of the domain count with their own
      $\theta_f$, so a body that cuts an inlet or a wall patch still has a closed control
      volume. The front and back faces of a two-dimensional case do not enter (they cancel).
\item The wall area $\Aw=|\Sw|$ and the wall normal $\nn_w=\Sw/\Aw$.
\item The \emph{wall distance} of the cell value from the wall,
      \begin{equation}
      \dw=\frac{\alpha_PV_P}{2\Aw}.
      \label{eq:dwall}
      \end{equation}
      It is exact for a fluid slab: if the fluid part of a cell is a layer of thickness
      $\delta$ on a wall of area $A$ parallel to a face, then $\alpha_PV_P=A\delta$,
      $\Aw=A$, and $\dw=\delta/2$ is the distance from the centroid of the layer to the wall.
      For a general cut it is an estimate of that distance from two integral quantities that
      the closure identity delivers exactly; Fig.~\ref{fig:walldist} compares it with the
      true centroid distance. For a fluid cell with one closed face on a Cartesian mesh it
      gives $h/2$, the exact distance of the cell centre from the face.
\item The \emph{no-slip (Dirichlet) coefficient}
      \begin{equation}
      \cns=\frac{\Aw}{\dw\,V_P}=\frac{2\Aw^2}{\alpha_PV_P^2}\qquad[\mathrm{m}^{-2}]
      \label{eq:cns}
      \end{equation}
      in wall cells of no-slip bodies (and of wall cells without a body), zero elsewhere.
      Multiplied by a diffusivity it is the wall exchange coefficient per unit volume.
\item The \emph{blanking coefficient} $b_P=\sum_f|\Sf|\delta_f/V_P$ in solid cells, zero
      elsewhere: the viscous diagonal per unit volume and diffusivity of an unobstructed cell.
\item The body index of every cut, solid and wall cell (Section~\ref{sec:bodies}).
\end{itemize}

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_walldist.pdf}
\caption{Wall distance. (a)~In a cut cell the first-order estimate \eqref{eq:dwall} is half
the thickness $\alpha V/\Aw$ of the slab with the same volume on the same wall area;

The second-order treatment of Section~\ref{sec:secondorder} uses $d_c$ itself.

\end{figure}

A closed triangulated surface (any OpenFOAM \code{searchableSurface} with an inside/outside

A body given as a fluid-fraction field $\alpha$ is intended for fractions produced by
another model (e.g.\ a deposit or a particle, re-read every step when the body is flagged as
moving).
Its cell fractions are the field values (clipped to $[0,1]$, optionally converted from a
solid fraction); point values are obtained by inverse-distance interpolation, $\alpha-\tfrac12$
serves as a point level set, edge crossings are found by linear interpolation of that level
set, and the same polygon construction gives $\theta_f$. This route smears the wall over about
one cell (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

The level sets of all bodies with a level set are combined by their minimum before the
fractions are computed, so overlapping bodies form an exact union; fraction-field bodies are
merged afterwards with the minimum of $\alpha$ and of $\theta$. Every cut or solid cell is
attributed to the body with the most negative level set at its centre (for a fraction-field
body: every cell whose fraction it lowered), and a wall cell that is not cut inherits the
body across its closed face. The attribution selects the wall condition, the wall
velocity, the
body to which the cell's force contribution is added%
, and the scalar wall condition%
. A wall cell without a body is treated as no-slip and its force is added to the first body.
\section{Discretisation for a body at rest}
\label{sec:static}

Every term of \eqref{eq:ns} is integrated over the fluid part $\Omega_P$ of each cell. The
boundary of $\Omega_P$ consists of the wet parts of the faces and of the wall segment. The
face parts are treated as in any finite-volume method, weighted by $\theta_f$; the wall
segment contributes to each term according to the boundary condition, and this is the only
place where the boundary condition enters. Table~\ref{tab:static} collects the result, and
the subsections derive each entry.

\subsection{Term-by-term derivation}

\paragraph{Unsteady term.} $\int_{\Omega_P}\partial_t\uu\dd V=\alpha_PV_P\,\dd\uu_P/\dd t$ for
a cell whose fluid volume does not change, discretised with implicit Euler. The cell value
$\uu_P$ is the average over the fluid part.

\paragraph{Convective term.} $\oint_{\partial\Omega_P}\uu(\uu\cdot\nn)\dd S$ receives nothing
from the wall, where $\uu\cdot\nn_w=0$ for a wall at rest under either condition. On the wet
face parts the mass flux is $\phi_f=\theta_f\,\bar\uu_f\cdot\Sf$ and the transported value is
the face interpolate (central differencing in all cases reported here, any OpenFOAM
convection scheme in general).

\paragraph{Viscous term.} $\oint\nu\nabla\uu\cdot\nn\dd S$ over the wet face parts is the
standard two-point flux weighted by $\theta_f$, with the diffusivity interpolated to the face
and multiplied by $\theta_f$,
\begin{equation}
\sum_f\theta_f\nu_f|\Sf|\,\frac{\uu_N-\uu_P}{|\bm d_f|}\quad
(+\text{ the usual non-orthogonal correction on non-orthogonal meshes}).
\label{eq:viscface}
\end{equation}
On the wall segment, free slip means zero tangential traction and, since $\uu\cdot\nn_w=0$,
no normal viscous flux either, so the wall contributes nothing. For no slip the wall shear is
approximated by a two-point difference between the cell value and the wall value $\bm 0$ over
the distance \eqref{eq:dwall},
\begin{equation}
\int_{\rm wall}\nu\nabla\uu\cdot\nn\dd S\simeq-\nu\Aw\frac{\uu_P-\bm 0}{\dw}
=-\nu\,\cns V_P\,\uu_P .
\label{eq:noslipwall}
\end{equation}
This is one implicit diagonal term per no-slip wall cell and per velocity component; the
components stay uncoupled because the difference is taken for the full vector (the normal
component is small anyway, being driven to zero by continuity).

\paragraph{Pressure term.} The volume-integrated gradient is
$\oint_{\partial\Omega_P}p\,\nn\dd S$; splitting the boundary into the wet faces and the wall
segment, and taking $p_w=p_P$ on the wall (first order),
\begin{equation}
\gradV p\big|_P\equiv\oint p\,\nn\dd S=\sum_f\theta_fp_f\Sf+p_P\Sw
\overset{\text{Prop.\,\ref{prop:closure}}}{=}\sum_f\theta_f\,(p_f-p_P)\,\Sf .
\label{eq:grad}
\end{equation}
The wall pressure force --- the form drag --- is therefore absorbed exactly into the face sum.
The momentum equation uses $\gradV p/V_P$, i.e.\ the gradient per unit \emph{cell} volume;
the factor $\alpha_P$ belongs to the mobility, not to the gradient (Section~\ref{sec:simplec}).

\paragraph{Continuity.} $\oint\uu\cdot\nn\dd S=\sum_f\phi_f+\int_{\rm wall}\uu\cdot\nn_w\dd S$,
and the wall integral vanishes for a wall at rest. Hence $\sum_f\phi_f=0$ with the same wet
fluxes as in the convective term.

\subsection{Properties}
\label{sec:staticprops}

The following properties hold to round-off because every wall-related quantity is built from
the same $\theta_f$.

\begin{proposition}[No force from a uniform pressure]
\label{prop:uniformp}
For $p\equiv p_0$, $\gradV p=\bm 0$ in every cell, cut or not.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$p_f-p_P=0$ in \eqref{eq:grad}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Free-stream divergence]
\label{prop:uniformu}
For a uniform discrete velocity $\uu\equiv\bm U$, $\sum_f\phi_f=-\bm U\cdot\Sw$ in every cell.
In particular the discrete divergence vanishes in a cut cell if and only if $\bm U\cdot\nn_w=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\sum_f\phi_f=\bm U\cdot\sum_f\theta_f\Sf=-\bm U\cdot\Sw$ by \eqref{eq:Sw}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[No-penetration is implied, not imposed]
\label{prop:nopen}
Let the velocity be locally uniform, $\uu=\bm U$, on the faces of a wall cell with
$\Aw\neq0$. Discrete continuity $\sum_f\phi_f=0$ holds if and only if $\bm U\cdot\nn_w=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from Proposition~\ref{prop:uniformu}. The statement holds for any shape of the wall
inside the cell, since only the area vector $\Sw$ enters.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:nopen} is the structural point of the method: the no-penetration
condition is never written down, it is a consequence of enforcing mass conservation on the cut
cell with fluxes and wall segment taken from the same fractions. The free-slip condition
appears only as the \emph{absence} of a wall term in the viscous operator, and the no-slip
condition as the presence of the single diagonal term $\nu\cns V_P$.

\begin{proposition}[Small cells strengthen the diagonal]
\label{prop:smallcell}
In a cut cell with a planar wall segment, the no-slip coefficient \eqref{eq:cns} grows like
$1/\alpha_P$ as $\alpha_P\to0$ at fixed $\Aw$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\cns=2\Aw^2/(\alpha_PV_P^2)$.
\end{proof}
The small-cell limit therefore drives $\uu_P$ onto the wall value and increases the
diagonal dominance of the momentum rows instead of degrading them; together with sliver
absorption it is what keeps the system well conditioned without cell merging. The price is
paid by the pressure (Section~\ref{sec:simplec}, and
Section~\ref{sec:smallcellp}%
).

\begin{proposition}[Discrete conservation]
\label{prop:conservation}
Summing the momentum equations of all cells, the convective and viscous face fluxes cancel in
pairs, and the pressure term reduces to the pressure force on the bodies,
$\sum_P\gradV p|_P=\sum_P p_P\bm S_{w,P}+(\text{domain boundary faces})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each internal face appears in the rows of its two cells with the same $\theta_f$ and opposite
$\Sf$. In \eqref{eq:grad} write $\sum_f\theta_fp_f\Sf+p_P\Sw$; the face terms cancel pairwise.
Solid cells have all faces closed and contribute nothing.
\end{proof}

Consequently the force on the bodies is the reaction to exactly the wall terms assembled in the
momentum equations (Section~\ref{sec:forces}), and at a steady state in a periodic domain the
total force equals the driving body force times the fluid volume. The same argument gives
global mass conservation, $\sum_P\sum_f\phi_f=0$, and it is what makes the pressure system
compatible (Section~\ref{sec:reference}).

Measured on manufactured solutions of Poisson and convection--diffusion problems on a disk,
the free-slip (Neumann) treatment is second-order accurate and the no-slip (Dirichlet)
treatment first-order accurate.
Section~\ref{sec:secondorder} shows that the first-order error comes mainly from the
diffusive fluxes through the faces of cut cells, which assume the two cell values to sit on the
cell centres, and only secondarily from the wall distance \eqref{eq:dwall}.

\section{Pressure--velocity coupling, forces and flow-rate forcing}
\label{sec:coupling}

\subsection{Solid cells}
\label{sec:solid}

Every face of a solid cell is closed, so its rows in the momentum and pressure matrices have
no off-diagonal entries and are decoupled from the fluid. They receive regular diagonals so
that the linear solvers see a well-posed system.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{Momentum.} The blanking diagonal $\nu\,b_PV_P=\nu\sum_f|\Sf|\delta_f$, the
      viscous diagonal of an unobstructed cell. The right-hand side is zero in a solid cell
      (the gradient \eqref{eq:grad} vanishes, the forcing is weighted with $\alpha_P=0$),
      and the blanking source $\nu b_PV_P\ub(\xx_P)$ is added, so $\uu_P=\ub$
      exactly, independently of the size of $b_P$.
\item \emph{Pressure.} The diagonal $-\bar a$, with $\bar a$ the mean magnitude of the
      diagonal over the non-solid cells, and a zero source, so $p_P=0$.
\end{itemize}
Neither value affects the fluid. The coefficient of the momentum row matters only for the
rate at which an iterative solver converges; its size makes the solid rows neither dominate nor
spoil the scaling of the matrix.

\subsection{SIMPLEC with cut-cell operators}
\label{sec:simplec}

Write the assembled momentum equation of cell $P$ as
\begin{equation}
a_P\uu_P=\bm H_P(\uu)-\gradV p|_P,\qquad
\bm H_P=\bm b_P-\sum_{nb}a_{nb}\uu_{nb},
\label{eq:mom}
\end{equation}
with $a_{nb}\le0$ the off-diagonal coefficients (OpenFOAM's sign convention), $\bm b_P$ the
explicit sources (old time level, wall and blanking sources, forcing) and $\gradV p$ the
volume-integrated cut-cell gradient \eqref{eq:grad}. With under-relaxation $\lambda_U$ the
diagonal is $a_P/\lambda_U$ and $\bm b_P$ holds the relaxation source. Define, per unit volume
as OpenFOAM does,
\begin{equation}
A_P=\frac{a_P}{V_P},\qquad H1_P=-\frac{1}{V_P}\sum_{nb}a_{nb},\qquad
r_{A}=\frac{1}{A_P}=\frac{V_P}{a_P},\qquad
r_{At}=\frac{1}{A_P-H1_P}=\frac{V_P}{a_P+\sum_{nb}a_{nb}} .
\label{eq:rA}
\end{equation}
$r_A$ is the mobility of SIMPLE and $r_{At}$ that of SIMPLEC, the response of the cell velocity
to a pressure gradient when the neighbours respond in the same way. Note that the momentum
equation keeps the full cell volume in $A$: the fluid volume enters only through the unsteady
diagonal $\alpha_PV_P/\dt$ and the wall term. The predictor is
$\bm H/a_P$, and the Rhie--Chow flux of the predictor is
\begin{equation}
\phi_{H,f}=\theta_f\,(r_A\bm H/V)_f\cdot\Sf
+\theta_f\,(r_{At}-r_A)_f\,|\Sf|\,\partial_np^{\rm old}_f
\;\big[+\,(r_A)_f\,\mathcal{D}_f\big],
\label{eq:phiH}
\end{equation}
with $\partial_n$ the face-normal gradient of the \code{snGrad} scheme and $\mathcal{D}_f$ the
transient Rhie--Chow correction (below). The pressure equation is the discrete divergence of
the corrected flux, with the Laplacian weighted by the same $\theta_f$ as the flux,
\begin{equation}
\sum_f\theta_f\,(r_{At})_f\,|\Sf|\,\partial_np_f=\sum_f\phi_{H,f}
\;+\;S_P
,
\qquad
\phi_f=\phi_{H,f}-\theta_f(r_{At})_f|\Sf|\,\partial_np_f ,
\label{eq:peqn}
\end{equation}
where $S_P$ is the mass source of a moving wall (zero for a body at rest,
Section~\ref{sec:moving}),
and the cell velocity is corrected with the cut-cell gradient,
$\uu_P=r_A\bm H_P/V_P-(r_{At})_P\gradV p|_P/V_P+\big((r_{At})_P-(r_A)_P\big)\gradV p^{\rm old}|_P/V_P$
(the last two terms combine into the familiar SIMPLEC update with
$\bm H/a_P$ modified by $(r_A-r_{At})\nabla p^{\rm old}$). Laplacian and flux in
\eqref{eq:peqn} carry the same $\theta_f$, so a closed face transports neither flux nor
pressure information, and $\phi_f$ is discretely divergence-free
(or equal to $-S_P$)
after the solve.

\paragraph{Floors of the mobility.} In a cut cell dominated by its wall term, $a_P$ can be
close to $\sum_{nb}|a_{nb}|$ and the SIMPLEC denominator $A-H1$ tiny or negative. It is
floored:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item transient solver: $A_P-H1_P\ge\alpha_P/\dt$, the unsteady diagonal of the cell's own
      fluid volume (zero in solid cells, where $A-H1=A>0$);
\item steady solver: $A_P-H1_P\ge10^{-3}A_P$, a pure safeguard.
\end{itemize}
$r_A$ itself is not floored. The reference implementation of Section~\ref{sec:verif} instead
floors $a_P$ at $\alpha_PV_P/\dt$; the difference is invisible in the converged transient
solutions but is one reason why the steady solver converges to a slightly different fixed
point (the Rhie--Chow dissipation depends on the relaxation).

\paragraph{Transient Rhie--Chow correction.} With \code{ddtCorr} active, the flux of the
predictor carries the difference between the old flux and the interpolate of the old
velocity,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}_f=c_f\,\frac{\phi^n_f-\theta_f\,\bar\uu^n_f\cdot\Sf}{\dt},\qquad
c_f=1-\min\!\Big(\frac{|\phi^n_f-\theta_f\bar\uu^n_f\cdot\Sf|}{|\phi^n_f|+\epsilon},1\Big),
\label{eq:ddtcorr}
\end{equation}
the Euler form of OpenFOAM's \code{ddtCorr} with $\theta$-weighted interpolation. It is added
to \eqref{eq:phiH} with the coefficient $(r_A)_f$.
For a moving body the old flux lives on the old wet faces and is first rescaled to the new
ones, $\phi^n_f\to\phi^n_f\,\theta^{n+1}_f/\theta^n_f$ where $\theta^n_f>0$, and replaced by
$\theta^{n+1}_f\bar\uu^n_f\cdot\Sf$ (no correction) on faces that have just opened. For a rigid
free stream the rescaled old flux equals the interpolated one and the correction vanishes.
Other time schemes fall back to the Euler form with a warning.

\subsection{Compatibility and the pressure reference}
\label{sec:reference}

The fluid region is a pure Neumann island for the pressure: no face of the fluid carries a
pressure boundary condition in a periodic domain, and the solid rows are decoupled. The
pressure Laplacian $L$ restricted to the fluid cells is therefore singular with null vector
$\bm 1$ and $\bm 1^{\!\top}L=\bm 0$. The reference is imposed as in
\code{fvMatrix::setReference}, by doubling one diagonal entry $d_r$ (and adding $d_rp_{\rm
ref}$ to the source) rather than replacing a row.

\begin{lemma}[Transparent reference]
\label{lem:reference}
Let $L'=L+d_r\bm e_r\bm e_r^{\!\top}$ and $L'p=\bm b$ (with $p_{\rm ref}=0$). Then
$d_rp_r=\bm 1^{\!\top}\bm b$. In particular, for a compatible right-hand side
($\bm 1^{\!\top}\bm b=0$) the reference is transparent, $p_r=0$ and $Lp=\bm b$ exactly; any
incompatibility is deposited at the reference cell as a point source.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Left-multiply $L'p=\bm b$ by $\bm 1^{\!\top}$ and use $\bm 1^{\!\top}L=\bm 0$.
\end{proof}

The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:peqn} is compatible because $\sum_P\sum_f\phi_{H,f}$
telescopes (each internal and coupled face appears twice with opposite sign)%
\ and $\sum_PS_P=0$ (Section~\ref{sec:primary})%
. In a domain with inflow and outflow boundaries the steady solver additionally balances the
boundary fluxes (\code{adjustPhi}). The reference cell must be a fluid cell; this is checked
at start-up.
For moving bodies the user's reference cell is kept while it is fluid and otherwise replaced by
the non-solid cell with the largest level set at its centre, i.e.\ the cell farthest from the
bodies, so that a body cannot swallow it (without a level set, the first fluid cell). The
pressure level then jumps; forces on closed bodies are unaffected because $\sum_P\bm S_{w,P}=\bm 0$
over a closed body.

\label{sec:algstatic}

is the same loop without the unsteady term, with one pass per iteration and relaxation of
$\uu$ and $p$.
The lines marked $\triangleright$ exist only when a body moves
(Section~\ref{sec:algo}).

\subsection{Force on the body}
\label{sec:forces}

By closure, the pressure force on a body is the sum of the wall terms of \eqref{eq:grad},
\begin{equation}
\bm F_p=\sum_{P\in\text{wall}}p_P\bm S_{w,P},\qquad
\bm F_v=\sum_{P\in\text{wall}}\nu\,\cns V_P\,(\uu_P
-\ub
),
\label{eq:forces}
\end{equation}
the viscous force being the reaction to the traction \eqref{eq:noslipwall} assembled in the
momentum equation (zero for free slip). Wall cells without a body are counted on the first
body. The forces are per unit density; for a two-dimensional case they refer to the depth of
the mesh, and are divided by it to obtain forces per unit depth. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation}, at a steady state in a periodic domain with forcing
$\bm g$ the total force equals $\bm g\sum_P\alpha_PV_P$ exactly; this identity is used as a
consistency check in Section~\ref{sec:verif}.

\subsection{Constant flow rate in periodic domains}
\label{sec:meanforce}

A periodic domain is driven by a uniform body force $\bm g=g\,\bm e$ adjusted so that the bulk
velocity $\bar u=\sum_P\alpha_PV_P\,\bm e\cdot\uu_P/V_f$, $V_f=\sum_P\alpha_PV_P$, equals a target
$U$. The force acts on the fluid only: the source of cell $P$ is $g\,\bm e\,\alpha_PV_P$.

\paragraph{Gain.} Adding a force increment $\delta g$ changes the cell velocity by
$\delta\uu_P=\bm e\,\rho_P\,\alpha_P\,\delta g$, where $\rho_P$ is the mobility of the cell to a
force that is the same in the neighbours. For a uniform increment the neighbour couplings
cancel in the momentum equation, so $\rho_P=1/(A_P-H1_P)=r_{At}$, the SIMPLEC mobility. The
bulk response is
\begin{equation}
\delta\bar u=\bar r\,\delta g,\qquad \bar r=\frac{\sum_P(r_{At})_P\,\alpha_P^2V_P}{V_f},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\delta g=\lambda\,\frac{U-\bar u}{\bar r},
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
with a relaxation $\lambda$ ($1$ by default); the extra factor $\alpha_P$ in $\bar r$ comes from
the force being weighted with $\alpha_P$ and the average with $\alpha_PV_P$. After every
momentum predictor and pressure correction, $\delta g$ is computed, added to the accumulated
increment, and the velocity is corrected by $\bm e\,(r_{At})_P\alpha_P\delta g$. The
stock OpenFOAM controller uses the gain $1/A$, which underestimates the bulk response by
the diffusion number $\nu\dt/h^2$ when the viscous term is implicit, and overshoots by two
orders of magnitude in the cases of Section~\ref{sec:verif}.
The mobility is floored at $A-H1\ge\max(10^{-3}A,\alpha_P/\dt)$ in transient runs, the second
bound covering the cells emerging from a moving body. The flow direction may be given
separately, so a zero target (a body moving through fluid at rest on average) is allowed.
\section{Moving bodies}
\label{sec:moving}

\subsection{What the wall sweeps}

The mesh never moves: no face carries a mesh velocity and no mesh-motion term appears in any
operator, so the method is not an arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian scheme. Only $\theta_f$,
$\alpha_P$ and $\Sw$ depend on time, and they are recomputed from the level set at every time
step. The entire modification lives on the wall segment.

Let $\Omega_P(t)$ be the fluid part of cell $P$. Its boundary consists of the wet parts of the
faces, which lie in fixed planes and sweep no volume, and the wall segment, which moves with
$\ub$. The Reynolds transport theorem for the volume of $\Omega_P(t)$ gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd(\alpha_PV_P)}{\dd t}=\int_{\rm wall}\ub\cdot\nn_w\dd S\simeq\ub\cdot\Sw ,
\label{eq:volume}
\end{equation}
and continuity over the same region, with $(\uu-\ub)\cdot\nn_w=0$ on the wall under either
condition,
\begin{equation}
0=\oint_{\partial\Omega_P}\uu\cdot\nn\dd S=\sum_f\phi_f+\int_{\rm wall}\ub\cdot\nn_w\dd S
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\;\sum_f\phi_f=-\int_{\rm wall}\ub\cdot\nn_w\dd S\equiv-S_P ,\;}
\label{eq:continuity}
\end{equation}
with $\phi_f=\theta_f\bar\uu_f\cdot\Sf$ the wet face flux as before. For a translation $\ub$ is
uniform and $S_P=\ub\cdot\Sw$ exactly; a rotation needs more care
(Section~\ref{sec:ubpoint}). The sign is as it must be:
a body advancing into the fluid has $\ub\cdot\nn_w<0$ ($\nn_w$ points into the body), the cell
loses volume, $S_P<0$, and the net outflow through its faces is positive.

\begin{table}[htbp]\centering\small
\caption{Every term for a body at rest and a body moving. Only three rows differ.}
\label{tab:moving}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
term & body at rest & body moving\\
\midrule
continuity & $\sum_f\phi_f=0$ & $\sum_f\phi_f=-S_P$, $\;S_P=\bm U\cdot\Sw+S^{\rm rot}_P$\\
convective & no wall term & no wall term\\
pressure & $p_P\Sw$, absorbed by closure & same\\
viscous, free slip & no wall term & no wall term\\
viscous, no slip & $\nu(\bm 0-\uu_P)\Aw/\dw$ & $\nu(\ub-\uu_P)\Aw/\dw$\\
unsteady & $\alpha_PV_P(\uu^{n+1}-\uu^n)/\dt$ & $(\alpha^{n+1}_P\uu^{n+1}-\alpha^n_P\uu^n)V_P/\dt$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:moving} lists every term. The convective row is the one that is easy to get
wrong: the absolute mass flux through the wall is not zero, but the momentum balance over the
\emph{time-varying} region is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int_{\Omega_P(t)}\uu\dd V+\oint_{\partial\Omega_P(t)}\uu\,(\uu-\bm w)\cdot\nn\dd S
=\dots,
\end{equation}
with $\bm w$ the velocity of the boundary: on the wall $\bm w=\ub$ and $(\uu-\ub)\cdot\nn=0$, on
the fixed faces $\bm w=\bm 0$ and the flux is the absolute one. So the convective operator is
untouched, provided the unsteady term is the two-level one of Table~\ref{tab:moving} with the
volume inside the difference. For free slip nothing else is added: repeating the argument of
Proposition~\ref{prop:nopen} with \eqref{eq:continuity} forces $\bm U\cdot\nn_w=\ub\cdot\nn_w$
for a locally uniform field --- \emph{the mass source is the boundary condition}. For no slip
the wall traction $\nu(\ub-\uu_P)\Aw/\dw$ keeps its diagonal half unchanged and acquires the
source $\nu\cns V_P\ub$, which vanishes for a wall at rest.

\subsection{Which quantity is primary}
\label{sec:primary}

Equations \eqref{eq:volume} and \eqref{eq:continuity} are the same statement, so on paper
either may be taken as the definition. Discretely the two choices are not equivalent, and
only one of them works.

\emph{Geometry first.} Compute $\alpha^n$ and $\alpha^{n+1}$ from the level set and set
$S_P=(\alpha^{n+1}_P-\alpha^n_P)V_P/\dt$. This differentiates a sampled quantity: the
sub-sampled $\alpha$ of \eqref{eq:subsample} carries a quadrature error of order $10^{-3}$ per
cell, absorption removes up to $\alpha_{\min}V_P$ in single steps, and divided by $\dt$ both
are amplified without bound. The resulting source is not compatible ($\sum_PS_P\neq0$), so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:reference} the whole defect is deposited at the pressure reference cell.
Figure~\ref{fig:gcl} quantifies both defects for a cylinder translating on a mesh: the
per-cell discrepancy between the two sources is of the order of the source itself, and the
domain sum of the geometric source is $10^{-2}$ instead of zero. In the reference
implementation this variant gives a free-stream error of $18\%$ of the body velocity at
$u_b=0.37$ and $90\%$ at $u_b=2$, which does not decrease with the number of outer iterations:
it is a wrong fixed point, not a splitting error.

\emph{Flux first.} Compute $\Sw$ from $\theta^{n+1}$, set
\begin{equation}
S_P=\bm U\cdot\Sw+S^{\rm rot}_P ,\qquad\text{then}\qquad
\alpha^n_PV_P:=\alpha^{n+1}_PV_P-\dt\,S_P ,
\label{eq:candB}
\end{equation}
with $\bm U$ the translational velocity of the body and $S^{\rm rot}_P$ the flux of its rotation
through the wall faces, \eqref{eq:srot} below (zero for a translating body).
Nothing is differentiated, and three properties follow.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{Consistency with the flux.} For a uniform $\uu\equiv\ub$ the discrete divergence
      equals $-S_P$ \emph{identically} (Proposition~\ref{prop:uniformu}), because $\Sw$ is
      assembled from the same $\theta_f$ that the flux uses.
\item \emph{Compatibility.} For the translational part, with $\bm U$ extended to all cells
      (its value off the wall cells is irrelevant since $\Sw=\bm 0$ there),
      \begin{equation}
      \sum_P\bm U\cdot\bm S_{w,P}=\sum_{f\,\rm internal}\theta_f\Sf\cdot(\bm U_N-\bm U_O)=0,
      \label{eq:sumS}
      \end{equation}
      with $O$ and $N$ the owner and neighbour of $f$: $\sum_PS_P$ is zero to round-off for
      every body position and every pattern of absorbed slivers (an absorbed cell has
      $\Sw=\bm 0$, coupled faces carry the same $\theta_f$ on both sides). The rotational part
      is a sum of face fluxes and telescopes in the same way (Proposition~\ref{prop:srot}). The
      sum is not exact when two bodies with different velocities share a cut cell or a face,
      or when a moving body is cut by a non-periodic domain boundary.
\item \emph{Geometric conservation law.} The second half of \eqref{eq:candB} is a backward
      Euler integration of \eqref{eq:volume}: the classical resolution of the discrete
      geometric conservation law, \emph{take the volume from the flux, not the flux from the
      volume}, applied per step. Since $\alpha^n$ is recomputed from the current geometry at
      every step and never accumulated, it cannot drift; it differs from the geometric
      fraction at the old time by $O(\dt^2)$ per step, i.e.\ it is first-order consistent.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Free-stream preservation]
\label{prop:freestream}
Let the body translate at a constant velocity $\ub$, let $\uu^n\equiv\ub$ in every cell
(including the solid) and $p\equiv0$, and let the flow-rate forcing be either absent or target
$\ub$. Then $\uu^{n+1}\equiv\ub$, $p\equiv0$ solves the discrete system exactly, for any $\dt$,
mesh and wall condition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the momentum row of a fluid cell the contributions for $\uu\equiv\ub$ are: unsteady
$(\alpha^{n+1}\ub-\alpha^n\ub)V_P/\dt$; convective $\ub\sum_f\phi_f=-S_P\ub$ (the flux having
been formed from $\ub$ through the new $\theta_f$, Section~\ref{sec:algo}); viscous zero, since
the $\theta$-weighted Laplacian annihilates a constant; no-slip wall
$\nu\cns V_P(\uu_P-\ub)=\bm 0$; pressure zero by Proposition~\ref{prop:uniformp}. The row
reduces to $\big((\alpha^{n+1}-\alpha^n)V_P/\dt-S_P\big)\ub=\bm 0$, which is \eqref{eq:candB}.
In solid cells $\uu=\ub$ is the blanking solution. Continuity holds because $\sum_f\phi_f=-S_P$
identically, so the pressure equation has zero right-hand side and $p\equiv0$; the transient
Rhie--Chow correction vanishes because the rescaled old flux is the free-stream flux.
\end{proof}

A rotating or accelerating body has no uniform exact solution, so the proposition does not
apply to it; the construction still guarantees consistency (item~1) and compatibility
(item~2).

\begin{remark}
With central differencing the diagonal contribution of the convective operator is
$\tfrac12\sum_f\phi_f=-\tfrac12S_P$, so once continuity is satisfied the unsteady and
convective terms together put $\tfrac12(\alpha^{n+1}+\alpha^n)V_P/\dt$ on the diagonal, the
\emph{average} of old and new fluid volume, and their row sum is $\alpha^n_PV_P/\dt$. A cell
just emerging from the body still carries half its new volume on the diagonal. In the first
outer iteration of a step the re-interpolated flux does not yet satisfy
\eqref{eq:continuity} unless the velocity is uniform, so these statements hold from the second
outer iteration on.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Where the body velocity is evaluated}
\label{sec:ubpoint}

For a translating body $\ub$ is uniform, $S_P=\bm U\cdot\Sw$ is exact and the question does not
arise. For a rotating one, $\ub(\xx)=\bm U+\bm\omega\times(\xx-\bm c)$ with $\bm c(t)$ the current
centre of rotation, no single evaluation point gives the flux through the wall.

\begin{lemma}[Rigid motion along the normal]
\label{lem:rignormal}
For any $\xx$ and $s$, $\ub(\xx+s\nn_w)\cdot\nn_w=\ub(\xx)\cdot\nn_w$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$(\bm\omega\times s\nn_w)\cdot\nn_w=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Planar surfaces]
\label{lem:planar}
For a planar surface $\Gamma$ with area vector $\bm S_\Gamma$ and centroid $\xx_\Gamma$ and any
field $q$ linear in $\xx$, $\int_\Gamma q\,\nn\dd S=q(\xx_\Gamma)\bm S_\Gamma$; in particular
$\int_\Gamma\ub\cdot\nn\dd S=\ub(\xx_\Gamma)\cdot\bm S_\Gamma$.
\end{lemma}

In two dimensions the wall of a cut cell is the chord between two edge crossings and $\Sw$ is
normal to it (Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}c). For a body of revolution turning about its axis the
chord midpoint $\xx_m$ lies on the line through $\bm c$ along $\Sw$, so the exact flux through the
chord vanishes, while a point evaluation gives
$\ub(\xx^e)\cdot\Sw=\bm\omega\cdot\big((\xx^e-\xx_m)\times\Sw\big)$, which by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rignormal} depends only on the offset of $\xx^e$ \emph{across} the normal. The
cell centre, the centre moved along the normal and the fluid centroid all lie off that line in
general: each leaves spurious local sources of order $|\bm\omega|h\Aw$, alternating in sign
around the body, which checkerboard the cut-cell pressure. (An earlier version of the code
evaluated $\ub$ at such a point; for a cylinder turning in place the source was non-zero in
every wall cell.)

The source is therefore split into the translational part, which is kept, and the flux of the
rotation computed from the faces:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;S_P=\bm U\cdot\Sw+S^{\rm rot}_P,\qquad
S^{\rm rot}_P=-\sum_{f\in P,\ \theta_f>0}\theta_f\,\big(\bm\omega\times(\xx'_f-\bm c)\big)\cdot\Sf ,\;}
\label{eq:srot}
\end{equation}
with $\Sf$ pointing out of $P$, $\theta_f$ the fraction after post-processing, and $\xx'_f$ the
centroid of the geometric wet part of the face (the fan-triangulated polygon of
Section~\ref{sec:geomcomp}, before absorption); in periodic directions $\xx'_f-\bm c$ is the minimum
image. Because $\bm\omega\times(\xx-\bm c)$ is linear and divergence-free, the divergence theorem
over the fluid part of the cell and Lemma~\ref{lem:planar} give
$S^{\rm rot}_P=\int_{\rm wall}\bm\omega\times(\xx-\bm c)\cdot\nn\dd S$ exactly, where the wall is
that of the post-processed cell: the chord(s) of the geometric cut and the geometric wet parts of
the faces closed by sliver absorption (Section~\ref{sec:absorb}). The translational part sees the
same wall, since $\Sw$ is formed with the post-processed $\theta_f$.

\begin{proposition}[Rotating-wall source]
\label{prop:srot}
The source \eqref{eq:srot}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item reduces to $\bm U\cdot\Sw$ for $\bm\omega=\bm 0$, so free-stream preservation
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:freestream}) and every translating case are unchanged;
\item is compatible for every rigid motion and every mesh, $\sum_PS_P=0$: every open face
      enters its two cells with opposite $\Sf$ and the same $\theta_f$ and $\xx'_f$, and the two
      copies of a periodic face map to the same minimum image about $\bm c$;
\item vanishes, for a body of revolution turning about its axis, in every wall cell not next to
      a cell removed by the post-processing; next to one it is the flux of the rotation through
      the closed face, which moves with the body like the rest of the wall.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\noindent Item~3 is a compromise. The purely geometric alternative (all geometrically wet faces,
with the flux of every removed cell handed to its fluid neighbours in proportion to the shared
wet area) vanishes in every cell of a body turning in place, but it leaves in a cell next to a
removed one the rotation flux through the closed face, $O(|\bm\omega|R\,\theta^{\rm geo}_fh)$,
which that cell's continuity, with no flux through a closed face, cannot carry; an area-weighted
share of the removed cell's flux does not cancel it face by face. The pressure takes up the
mismatch in cells of small mobility: in the Taylor--Couette case of
Section~\ref{sec:tc}
the cut-cell pressure errors at the rotating wall grew three- to sixfold%
, and with the second-order treatment the velocity lost its second order%
. With \eqref{eq:srot} the closed face is part of the moving wall, as in the translational part,
and the continuity of a cell is consistent with a velocity that moves with the body near the
wall. For a cylinder turning in place on an $81^2$ mesh, 5 of 63 wall cells carry a source.

The body velocity at a point is still needed for the \emph{tangential} wall velocity of the
no-slip traction $\nu(\ub-\uu_P)\Aw/\dw$, the viscous force and the blanking. Solid cells use their
centre and wall cells the point
\begin{equation}
\xx^e_P=\xx_{c,P}+\dw\,\nn_w ,
\label{eq:ubpoint}
\end{equation}
the foot of the normal from the fluid centroid $\xx_{c,P}$ (Section~\ref{sec:centroids}), which
is what the two-point wall gradient differences against; the centroid is computed whether or
not the second-order treatment is switched on.
For a nearly full cell with a very small wall segment, $\dw=\alpha_PV_P/(2\Aw)$ is an effective
distance that can exceed the cell size by orders of magnitude; taken literally, the point would
lie far outside the cell, in a periodic domain possibly beyond the minimum image. Since the wall
of such a cell lies inside it, the distance along $\nn_w$ is limited to where the normal leaves
the bounding box of the cell; the traction coefficient $\Aw/(\dw V_P)$ keeps $\dw$.

\subsection{Algorithm}
\label{sec:algo}

One time step (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pimple} and Fig.~\ref{fig:swept}):
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item Advance the time and move the bodies to $t^{n+1}$ (implicit in time); keep $\theta^n_f$
      for the transient Rhie--Chow correction.
\item Rebuild all geometry from scratch: $\theta_f$, $\alpha_P$ (with sliver absorption and
      coupled-face synchronisation), $\Sw$, $\Aw$, $\dw$, $\cns$, the cell sets and the body
      attribution%
      , the centroids and, if switched on, the second-order quantities%
      . The old geometry is not kept.
\item Evaluate $\ub$ in every body-owned cell (wall cells at \eqref{eq:ubpoint}, solid cells at
      their centre), form $S_P=\ub\cdot\Sw$ and $\alpha^n_PV_P=\alpha^{n+1}_PV_P-\dt S_P$, and
      store $\alpha^n$ as the old-time value of $\alpha$.
\item \emph{Re-interpolate the carried flux}: $\phi_f:=\theta_f^{n+1}\,\bar\uu^n_f\cdot\Sf$.
      The flux returned by the previous step lives on the faces of the \emph{old} cut mesh: a
      face that has just opened has no history there, and one that has just closed carries a
      history that no longer exists. The re-interpolated flux is only the initial guess of the
      convective operator --- the flux the step returns is the Rhie--Chow projected one either
      way --- but for a fluid translating rigidly with the body it lands exactly on the fixed
      point, which is why Proposition~\ref{prop:freestream} holds from the first outer
      iteration. Without it the fixed point is approached at roughly a factor of two per outer
      iteration. The previous Rhie--Chow flux is kept as the old-time flux for
      \eqref{eq:ddtcorr}.
\item Keep the pressure reference cell if it is fluid, otherwise relocate it
      (Section~\ref{sec:reference}).
\item For each of $n_{\rm outer}$ outer iterations: assemble and solve the momentum
      predictor with the sources $\alpha^nV_P\uu^n/\dt$, $\nu\cns V_P\ub$ (no slip) and the
      blanking source $\nu b_PV_P\ub$; form $\bm H/a_P$ and $\phi_H$; solve \eqref{eq:peqn}
      with $S_P$ on the right-hand side; correct flux and velocity. The geometry is frozen
      during the outer iterations.
\end{enumerate}
Equation \eqref{eq:peqn} with $S_P$ is the entire algorithmic modification of the pressure
equation. Newly uncovered cells enter the step with the velocity they had in the solid,
$\ub(\xx_P)$, and $p=0$. For this reason velocity and pressure are \emph{not} set to zero in
the solid at start-up when a body moves: a free stream must start with $\uu=\ub$ in the solid.

\caption{A cut cell over one step. The faces are fixed in space; only their wet fractions
change. The shaded band is the volume the wall sweeps, $\dt\,\ub\cdot\Sw=\dt S_P$ (negative

\subsection{Step restriction}

The row sum of the unsteady plus convective operator is $\alpha^n_PV_P/\dt$, which becomes
negative when the swept fraction does. By \eqref{eq:candB}, $\alpha^n_P<0$ when
\begin{equation}
\dt\,\ub\cdot\nn_w>\frac{\alpha^{n+1}_PV_P}{\Aw}=2\dw ,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ at a \emph{receding} wall (the rear of a body), in cells being uncovered whose linearised
swept volume exceeds their new fluid volume. This is a condition on the smallest wet cells,
hence set by $\alpha_{\min}$ rather than by the mesh spacing. It is not fatal when violated in
a few cells --- the diagonal there is still dominated by the viscous and wall terms, which grow
like $1/\alpha_P$ --- and the solver reports $\min_P\alpha^n_P$ and the number of cells with
$\alpha^n_P<0$ at every step (in the oscillating-cylinder case of Section~\ref{sec:galilean},
one or two cells in 25 of 400 steps, $\min_P\alpha^n_P=-0.032$, Fig.~\ref{fig:movingdiag}). At an advancing
staircase wall $\alpha^n_P$ can exceed $1$ by the same mechanism; this is harmless.

\subsection{Rigid body kinematics}

A body is defined at rest and moved by a rigid motion: a translation $\bm d(t)$ with velocity
$\bm U(t)$, and optionally a rotation with constant angular velocity $\bm\omega$ about the
body's reference centre $\bm c_0$ (the centre of an analytical body, the bounding-box centre
of a triangulated one, or the point given as \code{centreOfRotation} in the motion
dictionary), by the angle $|\bm\omega|t$ measured from $t=0$.
Table~\ref{tab:motions} lists the motions. The velocity must be the exact derivative of the
displacement: the first supplies the wall condition, the second, through the geometry, the
mass source, and if they disagree the two halves of the boundary condition pull against each
other (this is not checked for prescribed functions).

\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
type & displacement $\bm d(t)$ & velocity $\bm U(t)$\\
\midrule

Level-set queries transform the query point into the body frame. With $\bm c(t)=\bm c_0+\bm
d(t)$ and periodic lengths $L_k$ (given in the geometry dictionary),
\begin{equation}
\xx_{\rm body}=\bm c_0+\mathsf R(-|\bm\omega|t)\,\big[\,\mathrm{mi}(\xx-\bm c(t))\,\big],\qquad
\mathrm{mi}(r)_k=r_k-L_k\big\lfloor r_k/L_k+\tfrac12\big\rfloor ,
\end{equation}
the minimum image of the point about the current centre in every periodic direction, so that
a body may cross a periodic boundary. The same transform gives $\ub(\xx)=\bm U+\bm\omega\times
\mathrm{mi}(\xx-\bm c(t))$. The \code{external} motion is the hook for a body driven by its
own equation of motion; a body given as a fraction field and flagged as moving has its field
re-read every step and its velocity taken from a supplied velocity field or a uniform value.
\section{Passive scalar transport}
\label{sec:scalar}

\subsection{Discrete equation}

Integrating \eqref{eq:scalar} over the fluid part of a cell with the same arguments as for
momentum gives, for each scalar $T$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{(\alpha^{n+1}_PT^{n+1}_P-\alpha^n_PT^n_P)V_P}{\dt}
+\sum_f\phi_f\bar T_f
-\sum_f\theta_fD\,\delta_f|\Sf|(T_N-T_P)\\
&\qquad+D\,c_{w,P}V_P\,(T_P-T_{w,P})+D\,b_PV_P\,(T_P-T_{w,P})
=q_P\,\alpha^{n+1}_PV_P+s_P V_P ,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:scalard}
\end{equation}
where every $T$ without superscript is at $t^{n+1}$ and
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item $\phi_f$ is the final wet flux of the flow solver, so the scalar is convected by exactly
      the flux that satisfies discrete continuity%
      \ ($\sum_f\phi_f=-S_P$ for a moving body)%
      ; the convection scheme is that of the case (central in all cases reported here);
\item the diffusive face flux is $\theta_f$-weighted as for momentum, with the constant
      diffusivity $D$ of the scalar (plus the non-orthogonal correction of the Laplacian
      scheme);
\item $c_{w,P}=\Aw/(\dw V_P)=2\Aw^2/(\alpha_PV_P^2)$ is the wall coefficient \eqref{eq:cns},
      applied in the wall cells of the bodies that are fixed-value \emph{for this scalar},
      with $T_{w,P}$ the value of the attributed body; it is zero in the wall cells of
      zero-flux bodies;
\item $b_P$ is the blanking coefficient of the solid cells, where $T_{w,P}$ is the value of the
      owning body if it is fixed-value for this scalar and $0$ otherwise;
\item $q_P$ is an optional source per unit fluid volume, uniform in a disk (two dimensions) or
      sphere (three dimensions) of given centre and radius, set in the cells whose centre lies
      inside it; it multiplies $\alpha^{n+1}_P$ and never injects into the solid;
\item $s_P$ collects the sources and sinks of the finite-volume options (\code{fvOptions})
      that list the scalar, per unit \emph{cell} volume as OpenFOAM applies them; an implicit
      coefficient enters the diagonal, an explicit one the source;
\item $\alpha^n_P$ is the swept fraction \eqref{eq:candB} for a moving body and
      $\alpha^n_P=\alpha^{n+1}_P$ for a body at rest.
\end{itemize}

The wall segment is seen either as a fixed-value surface --- the analogue of no slip, one
diagonal term $Dc_wV_P$ and the source $Dc_wV_PT_w$, i.e.\ the flux $D\Aw(T_P-T_w)/\dw$ through
the wall --- or as a zero-flux surface, the analogue of free slip, which needs no term at all:
the wall segment is not a mesh face and simply carries no flux. The two masks, for velocity and
scalars, are independent, and each scalar has its own. Cells containing the wall are fluid
cells: the scalar enters their fluid part through the wet fractions of their faces and never
crosses the wall segment. There is no convective wall term, because the relative flux through
the wall vanishes%
; in particular no mass-source term appears for a moving body, the effect of the
moving wall being carried entirely by the two-level unsteady term%
. The solid rows are decoupled (all faces closed, $\alpha^{n+1}=\alpha^n=0$) and reduce to
$Db_PV_P(T_P-T_{w,P})=s_PV_P$, so $T_P=T_{w,P}$ exactly when no option acts there; a cell
released by a moving body starts from this defined value.

In OpenFOAM the equation is assembled as
\begin{lstlisting}
fvm::ddt(ibm.alpha(), T) + fvm::div(phi, T) - fvm::laplacian(DT*ibm.theta(), T)
+ fvm::Sp(DT*wallCoeff, T) + fvm::Sp(DT*ibm.blankCoeff(), T)
== DT*(wallCoeff + ibm.blankCoeff())*Twall + q*ibm.alpha() + fvOptions(T)
\end{lstlisting}
with \code{wallCoeff} and \code{Twall} the per-scalar masks. The transient solver solves the
scalars once per time step, after the last outer iteration, with the final flux; the steady
solver once per SIMPLE iteration, after the pressure correction.

\subsection{Discrete conservation}

\begin{proposition}[Scalar budget]
\label{prop:budget}
In a periodic or closed domain, with no finite-volume option acting in solid cells and the
linear systems solved exactly, the scalar content $C^n=\sum_P\alpha^n_{g,P}V_PT^n_P$, with
$\alpha_g$ the geometric fraction of the current step, satisfies
\begin{equation}
C^{n+1}-C^n=\dt\sum_P\big(q_P\alpha^{n+1}_P+s_P\big)V_P
-\dt\sum_PDc_{w,P}V_P\big(T^{n+1}_P-T_{w,P}\big)
-\sum_P\big(\alpha^n_{g,P}-\alpha^n_P\big)V_PT^n_P
.
\label{eq:budgetstep}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Sum \eqref{eq:scalard} over all cells. Every internal and coupled face appears in the rows of
its two cells with the same flux and the same diffusive coefficient and opposite sign, whatever
the interpolation of $\bar T_f$, so the convective and diffusive sums vanish; the wall is not a
face. The solid rows balance the blanking term against the options acting there, which are zero
by assumption. What remains is
$\sum_P(\alpha^{n+1}_PT^{n+1}_P-\alpha^n_PT^n_P)V_P/\dt=\sum_P(q\alpha^{n+1}+s)V_P-
\sum_PDc_wV_P(T-T_w)$, and $\sum_P\alpha^n_PV_PT^n_P=C^n-\sum_P(\alpha^n_g-\alpha^n)V_PT^n$.
\end{proof}

Summed over the steps, \eqref{eq:budgetstep} is the budget
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\underbrace{C^n-C^0}_{\text{content}}
-\underbrace{\sum_m\dt\sum_P(q_P\alpha_P+s_P)V_P}_{\text{injected}}
+\underbrace{\sum_m\dt\sum_PDc_{w,P}V_P(T_P-T_{w,P})}_{\text{through walls}}\\
&\qquad+\underbrace{\sum_m\sum_P(\alpha^{\rm prev}_P-\alpha^m_P)V_PT^m_P}_{\text{swept defect}}
&\qquad=\text{round-off},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
which the solver evaluates and reports at every step, with the options' contribution computed
exactly as the matrix applied it (implicit part times the new value, explicit part as
assembled). The residual is the accumulated linear-solver tolerance. The steady solver reports
the balance of rates instead, $\sum_P(q\alpha+s)V_P$ against $\sum_PDc_wV_P(T-T_w)$.
The swept-defect term exists only for moving bodies: $\alpha^n$ is integrated from the wall
flux and is first-order consistent with the geometric fraction $\alpha^{\rm prev}$ of the
previous step, and a cell absorbed into the body hands over no content. It is the price of the
exact geometric conservation law of Section~\ref{sec:primary}; it is of either sign, of
order $\dt$, and small (Section~\ref{sec:verifscalar}).
Fluxes through non-periodic domain boundaries are not included in the reported budget;
all cases reported here are periodic.

\subsection{Consistency and boundedness}

Conservation and consistency need different conditions. A uniform field $T\equiv T_0$ with
$q=s=0$ is preserved by \eqref{eq:scalard} when every wall term vanishes, i.e.\ when the
fixed-value walls have $T_w=T_0$: the convective row sum is $T_0\sum_f\phi_f$, which cancels
against the unsteady term by
\eqref{eq:candB} and \eqref{eq:continuity} for a moving body (Proposition~\ref{prop:freestream}),
to the tolerance of the pressure solver. For a moving zero-flux body a released cell
comes from the solid with the blanked value $0$, so a uniform $T_0\neq0$ is perturbed
there by $O(\alpha^n_PT_0)$, the same effect as the swept defect.

With central differencing the off-diagonal coefficient of face $f$ is
$\tfrac12\phi_f-\theta_fD\delta_f|\Sf|$. The factor $\theta_f$ appears in both terms, so the
condition for non-positive off-diagonals, the face P\'eclet number
$|\bar\uu_f\cdot\Sf|/(D\delta_f|\Sf|)<2$, is the same in cut cells as in full ones. The diagonal
is $\alpha^{n+1}V/\dt$ (%
$\tfrac12(\alpha^{n+1}+\alpha^n)V/\dt$ for a moving body, %
plus the neighbour sum) and the wall coefficient, which grows like $1/\alpha_P$ in small cells
(Proposition~\ref{prop:smallcell}). The matrix is then an M-matrix and a discrete maximum
principle, $\min(T^n,T_w)\le T^{n+1}\le\max(T^n,T_w)$ for $q=s=0$, holds%
\ provided $\alpha^n\ge0$, i.e.\ the step restriction of Section~\ref{sec:moving} is met%
. In the cases below the cell P\'eclet number is about $0.6$. A bounded convection scheme
(upwind or TVD) keeps conservation and consistency, since both rely only on the flux.
In a steady problem with zero-flux walls only and a net source there is no steady solution;
with fixed-value walls the level of the solution is set by the wall sink.

\subsection{Several scalars and input}

The scalars are listed in \code{constant/scalarTransportProperties} and solved in turn, each
with its own diffusivity, source, wall conditions, options and budget:
\begin{lstlisting}
scalars
{
    T { DT 0.1; source { centre (5 8.5 0); radius 0.5; rate 1; }
        walls { cylinder { type fixedValue; value 0; } ".*" { type zeroGradient; } } }
    c { DT 0.01; }                       // walls from the body entries of ibmProperties
}
\end{lstlisting}
The body names in \code{walls} may be regular expressions; a body not listed falls back to its
own \code{scalarWall} (default \code{zeroGradient}) and \code{scalarWallValue} entries in
\code{constant/ibmProperties}. An older single-scalar form (the entries of one scalar at the
top level of the file) is still accepted. The coupling is one-way: the flow does not see the
scalars, and the scalars do not see each other except through options that read other fields,
which are then lagged by the sequential solution.
\section{Second-order wall treatment}
\label{sec:secondorder}

The treatment of Sections~\ref{sec:static}--\ref{sec:coupling} is first order for no-slip and
fixed-value walls. This section derives an optional treatment, switched on by
\code{secondOrder true} in \code{constant/ibmProperties}, that makes the diffusive operators
and the velocity second order. It rests on one principle --- the value of a cut cell lives at
the centroid of its fluid part --- and on two ingredients: a diffusive face flux consistent
with the centroids, and the wall distance of the centroid. Neither needs a ghost value, a
probe into the fluid or a larger stencil.

\subsection{Cell values live at fluid centroids}
\label{sec:centroids}

For a smooth field, the average over the fluid part of a cell equals the point value at its
centroid to second order,
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{\alpha_PV_P}\int_{\Omega_P}u\dd V=u(\xx_{c,P})+O(h^2),\qquad
\xx_{c,P}=\frac{1}{\alpha_PV_P}\int_{\Omega_P}\xx\dd V ,
\end{equation}
because the linear term of the Taylor expansion about $\xx_c$ integrates to zero. In a full
cell $\xx_c=\xx_P$ and nothing changes; in a cut cell the centroid is displaced by $O(h)$
from the cell centre, and every operator that treats $u_P$ as the value at $\xx_P$ makes an
$O(h)$ error in position. Two centroids are computed (Fig.~\ref{fig:centroids}):
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item the \emph{fluid centroid} $\xx_{c,P}=\xx_P+\bm o_P$ as the mean position of the fluid
      samples of the sub-sampling that gives $\alpha_P$ \eqref{eq:subsample}, with error
      $O(h/n_s)$; it is computed for every candidate cell (and set to $\xx_P$ in full and solid
      cells), whether or not the switch is on;
\item the \emph{wet-face centroid} $\xx'_f=\xx_f+\bm o'_f$ as the centroid of the fluid polygon
      of Section~\ref{sec:geomcomp}, by fan triangulation, $\xx'_f=\sum_i|\bm a_i|(\bm p_0+\bm
      p_i+\bm p_{i+1})/(3\sum_i|\bm a_i|)$, exact for the polygon.
\end{itemize}
Fraction-field bodies have no sub-samples and keep $\bm o_P=\bm 0$.

\subsection{Why the no-slip wall is first order}

Consider the diffusive flux density through face $f$ between $P$ and $N$, discretised as
$(u_N-u_P)\delta_f$ with $\delta_f=1/h$ on a uniform Cartesian mesh (variant F0). The values
live at the centroids, separated by
\begin{equation}
\bm d=\xx_{c,N}-\xx_{c,P}=h\,\nn+(\bm o_N-\bm o_P).
\end{equation}
Expanding about the midpoint $\bm m$ of the two centroids,
$u_N-u_P=\nabla u(\bm m)\cdot\bm d+O(|\bm d|^3)$, so
\begin{equation}
\frac{u_N-u_P}{h}=\nn\cdot\nabla u(\bm m)+\frac{(\bm o_N-\bm o_P)\cdot\nabla u(\bm m)}{h}+O(h^2).
\label{eq:F0error}
\end{equation}
The second term is an $O(1)$ error in the flux density on every face of the layer of cut cells,
since the offsets are $O(h)$. Its size depends on the wall condition. The offsets of cut cells
are predominantly normal to the wall: at a Neumann wall the normal derivative vanishes, so
$(\bm o_N-\bm o_P)\cdot\nabla u=O(h)\cdot O(h)$ and the error is $O(h)$ per unit flux density;
at a Dirichlet wall the normal derivative is $O(1)$ and the error is $O(1)$. This is the
observation of the order study summarised in Fig.~\ref{fig:order_ablation}: the Neumann problems
are second order with the original scheme, the Dirichlet problems first order, and improving
only the wall distance (V1/F0) leaves them first order. The wall distance
\eqref{eq:dwall} is itself inconsistent for inclined walls (for the corner triangle
$(0,0),(h,0),(0,h)$ it gives $0.177h$ against the true centroid distance $0.236h$, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:walldist}b shows the spread), but that is not the limiting error.

\subsection{Centroid-consistent diffusive flux}
\label{sec:F1}

Decompose the face normal as
\begin{equation}
\nn=\frac{\bm d}{\nn\cdot\bm d}+\bm k,\qquad \bm k=\nn-\frac{\bm d}{\nn\cdot\bm d},\quad
\bm k\cdot\nn=0 ,
\end{equation}
so that $\bm k$ lies in the plane of the face (Fig.~\ref{fig:centroids}). Then
$\nn\cdot\nabla u=\bm d\cdot\nabla u/(\nn\cdot\bm d)+\bm k\cdot\nabla u$, and the first part
is approximated by the two-point difference along $\bm d$, which is centred between the two
values, while the second is evaluated with a reconstructed gradient $\bm g_f$:
\begin{equation}
\nn\cdot\nabla u\big|_f\simeq c_f\,(u_N-u_P)+\Big[\bm g_f\cdot\nn-c_f\,\bm g_f\cdot\bm d\Big],
\qquad c_f=\frac{1}{\max(\nn\cdot\bm d,\,0.1\,h_f)} ,
\label{eq:f1}
\end{equation}
with $h_f$ the mesh distance across the face. This is the over-relaxed non-orthogonal
correction of finite-volume methods, applied to the centroids instead of the cell centres. The
flux through the wet part of the face is
\begin{equation}
F_f=\theta_f\,\Gamma_f\,|\Sf|\Big\{c_f(u_N-u_P)+\big[\bm g_f\cdot\nn-c_f\,\bm g_f\cdot\bm d\big]\Big\},
\label{eq:F1flux}
\end{equation}
with the first term implicit and the bracket explicit (deferred correction, re-evaluated at
every assembly, i.e.\ once per outer iteration). The ingredients are:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item $\bm d=\bm d_f+\bm o_N-\bm o_P$ on internal and coupled faces that are open and
      between two non-solid cells; on all other faces the mesh values (which makes the
      correction vanish on faces between full cells of an orthogonal mesh);
\item the \emph{least-squares gradient on the centroids},
      \begin{equation}
      \bm g_P=\mathsf A_P^{-1}\sum_{N}w_{PN}\,\bm d_{PN}(u_N-u_P),\qquad
      \mathsf A_P=\sum_Nw_{PN}\,\bm d_{PN}\bm d_{PN}^{\!\top},\quad w_{PN}=|\bm d_{PN}|^{-2},
      \label{eq:lsq}
      \end{equation}
      over the neighbours across open faces (the wall value is not used), with a unit diagonal
      added for each non-solved direction; $\bm g_P=\bm 0$ if $\mathsf A_P$ is singular (e.g.\
      all open neighbours collinear);
\item the face gradient $\bm g_f=\tfrac12(\bm g_P+\bm g_N)$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Exactness for linear fields]
\label{prop:linear}
If $u=a+\bm G\cdot\xx$ and $\mathsf A_P,\mathsf A_N$ are non-singular, then
\eqref{eq:f1} returns $\nn\cdot\bm G$ exactly, independently of the floor on $\nn\cdot\bm d$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The least-squares right-hand side is $\sum_Nw\bm d(\bm d\cdot\bm G)=\mathsf A_P\bm G$, so
$\bm g_P=\bm g_N=\bm g_f=\bm G$. Then
$c_f\,\bm G\cdot\bm d+\bm G\cdot\nn-c_f\,\bm G\cdot\bm d=\bm G\cdot\nn$; the same $c_f$ multiplies
the implicit and the subtracted explicit term, so the floor cancels.
\end{proof}

\noindent Further properties follow directly.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{Conservation.} $\nn$ and $\bm d$ change sign with the orientation of the face, so
      the flux \eqref{eq:F1flux} is antisymmetric and the discrete conservation of
      Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation} is kept (also across coupled faces).
\item \emph{Uniform and linear fields} are reproduced, so the free-stream property of a body at
      rest%
      \ and Proposition~\ref{prop:freestream}
      \ are unaffected, and so are closure, blanking, the mass source and the flow-rate
      forcing.
\item \emph{Stability.} The implicit part has coefficients $\theta_f\Gamma_f|\Sf|c_f>0$ and is
      an M-matrix; the floor keeps $c_f\le10/h_f$. The explicit remainder vanishes where
      $\bm d\parallel\nn$.
\item \emph{Accuracy.} $\bm g_f=\nabla u(\bm m)+O(h)$ and $|\bm k|=O(1)$ in cut cells, so the flux
      density error on cut faces is $O(h)$ instead of the $O(1)$ of \eqref{eq:F0error}; on the
      $O(h^{1-d})$ faces of the cut layer this is the classical situation in which a local
      truncation error of first order in a codimension-one set still yields a second-order
      global error. The order study confirms it (Section~\ref{sec:verifsecond}).
\end{itemize}

In OpenFOAM, \eqref{eq:f1} is one \code{snGradScheme}, \code{cutCellCorrected}: its delta
coefficients are $c_f$ and its explicit correction the bracket. Selected for
\code{laplacianSchemes} and \code{snGradSchemes}, it is used consistently by the Gauss
Laplacian of the momentum and pressure equations (the correction moves to the source), by
\code{pEqn.flux()}, and by \code{fvc::snGrad} in the SIMPLEC term of \eqref{eq:phiH}, so the flux
and velocity corrections stay consistent without any change to the solvers.
The scalar equations inherit it through their Laplacian scheme.

\subsection{Wall flux with the centroid distance}

In the no-slip term \eqref{eq:noslipwall} (and the fixed-value scalar term) the distance
$\dw$ of \eqref{eq:dwall} is replaced by the distance of the fluid centroid from the wall,
\begin{equation}
d_c=\max\big(\varphi(\xx_{c,P}),\;10^{-3}V_P^{1/3}\big),
\label{eq:dc}
\end{equation}
evaluated with the union level set (V1). It is the exact centroid distance for level sets that
are signed distances (the analytical bodies and triangulated surfaces); fraction-field bodies
keep \eqref{eq:dwall}. For a fluid cell behind a closed face it is the distance to the true
surface rather than to the closed face. The two-point wall flux
\begin{equation}
\Gamma\Aw\frac{u_P-u_w}{d_c}=\Gamma\Aw\Big(\partial_nu|_w+\tfrac12d_c\,\partial^2_nu|_w+\dots\Big)
\end{equation}
has an $O(h)$ error in flux density on an area $O(h^{d-1})$, the same class as \eqref{eq:f1},
and suffices for second order. A three-point normal probe (V3) gives no improvement, and
scaling the wall row with the cell's Laplacian (V2) is inconsistent, because the cell
Laplacian contains the tangential curvature (Fig.~\ref{fig:order_ablation}). The same $d_c$
enters the viscous force%
\ and the evaluation point \eqref{eq:ubpoint} of the wall velocity%
.

\subsection{Pressure gradient and forces}
\label{sec:grad2}

With the switch on, the cut-cell gradient \eqref{eq:grad} is made exact for a linear pressure.
Let $\bm g$ be the least-squares gradient \eqref{eq:lsq} of $p$ on the centroids, $\bm g_f=
w_f\bm g_O+(1-w_f)\bm g_N$ its face interpolate, and
$\bm m_f=w_f\xx_{c,O}+(1-w_f)\xx_{c,N}$ the point the linear interpolate
$\bar p_f=w_fp_O+(1-w_f)p_N$ of centroid values belongs to. Then
\begin{equation}
\gradV p\big|_P=\sum_f\theta_f\Big[\bar p_f+\bm g_f\cdot(\xx'_f-\bm m_f)\Big]\Sf
+\sum_k\Big[p_P+\bm g_P\cdot(\xx_k-\xx_{c,P})\Big]\bm S_k .
\label{eq:grad2}
\end{equation}
The face correction moves the face value from $\bm m_f$ to the wet-face centroid $\xx'_f$; it is
applied on every open face of a cell with a centroid offset and on every partially wet face, and
vanishes between two full cells of an orthogonal mesh ($\bm m_f=\xx'_f=\xx_f$). The wall of the cell
is split into planar pieces $k$ with area vector $\bm S_k$ (out of the fluid) and centroid
$\xx_k$:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item the wall polygon(s) of the geometric cut. On every cut face of the cell the wall trace runs
      from the crossing where the face boundary leaves the fluid to the next crossing; oriented
      by the face (reversed for the owner, whose $\Sf$ points out of the cell), the traces chain
      into closed loops, which are fan-triangulated about their mean point. In two dimensions a
      loop is the chord times the depth; in three dimensions it is the polygon of the cut-edge
      crossings, not planar in general, and the triangles are summed individually;
\item the geometric wet part of every face closed by the post-processing, area vector
      $(\theta^{\rm geo}_f-\theta_f)\Sf$ at the wet-face centroid.
\end{itemize}
By the closure of the geometric fluid polyhedron $\sum_k\bm S_k=\Sw$, which the code checks
(the mismatch is $10^{-11}$ or less; a cell whose traces do not close would fall back on the
extrapolation to the normal foot of the centroid, which has not occurred). Only
$\sum_k(\xx_k-\xx_{c,P})\bm S_k$ is stored, one tensor per wall cell, and the wall term is
$p_P\Sw+\bm g_P\cdot\sum_k(\xx_k-\xx_{c,P})\bm S_k$.

\begin{proposition}[Linearly exact gradient]
\label{prop:grad2}
If the least-squares gradient is exact for linear fields, \eqref{eq:grad2} gives
$\gradV p|_P=V^{\rm geo}_P\bm G$ for every $p=p_0+\bm G\cdot\xx$ and every cell, with
$V^{\rm geo}_P$ the volume of the geometric fluid polyhedron. It vanishes for a uniform $p$,
conserves momentum, and the force $\bm F_p=\sum_P\sum_kp_k\bm S_k$ keeps the momentum-sum
identity.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each face term is $p(\xx'_f)\theta_f\Sf$, the exact integral over the planar wet face
(Lemma~\ref{lem:planar}); each wall term is $p(\xx_k)\bm S_k$, exact over a planar piece. The wet
faces and the pieces bound the geometric polyhedron, and the divergence theorem gives
$V^{\rm geo}_P\bm G$. A uniform $p$ gives $p(\sum_f\theta_f\Sf+\sum_k\bm S_k)=\bm 0$; the face
corrections are antisymmetric because $\xx'_f$ and $\bm m_f$ are shared by both cells.
\end{proof}

The previous form corrected only partially wet faces, by $\bm g_f\cdot(\xx'_f-\xx_f)$, and took
the wall value at the normal foot of the centroid, $p_c+d\,\bm g\cdot\nn_w$; for a linear pressure
its relative error in the wall cells was of order one ($L_2$ $0.74$--$0.98$,
Section~\ref{sec:verifsecond}). The
least-squares fit needs as many fluid neighbours as dimensions; a cell with fewer (a sliver in
three dimensions) takes the mean gradient of its fluid face neighbours, which keeps
Proposition~\ref{prop:grad2}. The force \eqref{eq:forces} uses the same pieces. Since
$\alpha_PV_P$ and $V^{\rm geo}_P$ differ by the sub-sampling error of $\alpha$
(Section~\ref{sec:geomcomp}), a fluid at rest under a uniform body force $\alpha V\bm g$ is in
balance up to that error only; weighting the body force with $V^{\rm geo}$, or computing $\alpha$
from the polyhedron, would make it exact.

\subsection{The small-cell problem of the pressure}
\label{sec:smallcellp}

The operators and the velocity become second order with the two ingredients above, but the
pressure of the cut cells does not converge (Section~\ref{sec:verifsecond}). The reason is the
scaling of the mobility. In a thin cut cell the momentum diagonal is dominated by the wall term,
\begin{equation}
a_P\simeq\nu\Aw/d_c\propto\nu\Aw^2/(\alpha_PV_P),\qquad
r_{A,P}=V_P/a_P\propto\alpha_P\,V_P^2/(\nu\Aw^2),
\end{equation}
against $r_A\sim h^2/\nu$ in a full cell, while the pressure row of the cut cell has the
diagonal $\sum_f\theta_f(r_{At})_f|\Sf|c_f$, which scales with the wet fractions. The cut-cell
pressure is thus determined by small flux residuals divided by a small coefficient, and the
cell's own velocity barely responds to it, being pinned by the wall term. Through the face
values $p_f$ the error feeds back into the neighbours' momentum and pollutes the near-wall
pressure, and with it the forces, at first order. The velocity is hardly affected. The
consistency of the gradient is not the cause: with the linearly exact gradient of
Section~\ref{sec:grad2} the Taylor--Couette pressure errors change by at most $7\%$
(Section~\ref{sec:tc}). A cut-cell pressure that is not determined by the cut cell alone ---
cell merging or linking of cut cells below $\alpha\approx0.5$ to a full neighbour, or a wall
pressure formed from the full cells --- is the natural next step.

\subsection{Implementation and scope}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item Switch and schemes: \code{secondOrder true} in \code{constant/ibmProperties}, and
\begin{lstlisting}
laplacianSchemes { default Gauss linear cutCellCorrected; }
snGradSchemes    { default cutCellCorrected; }
\end{lstlisting}
      Mismatched settings are not detected: \code{cutCellCorrected} with the switch off is the
      \code{uncorrected} scheme (identical to \code{corrected} on orthogonal meshes), and the
      switch with \code{corrected} gives V1/F0, which is still first order.
\item With the switch off, $\theta$, $\alpha$, $\dw$, the gradient, the forces and the schemes
      are bit-identical to the first-order treatment.
      The changes on this branch that are not behind the switch concern rotating
      bodies only: the evaluation point \eqref{eq:ubpoint} of the wall velocity, which
      uses the centroid in both cases, and the rotational source \eqref{eq:srot}.
\item The convective face value is not reconstructed at the wet-face centroid: in the order
      study this refinement (C1) changes the error by 10--30\% on coarse meshes and nothing
      asymptotically, and convection uses the scheme of the case.
\item For moving bodies all centroid quantities and wall pieces are recomputed with the
      geometry at every step. The combination has been exercised only with a body rotating in
      place (Section~\ref{sec:tc}).
\item The scalars obtain \eqref{eq:f1} and \eqref{eq:dc} through the Laplacian scheme and the
      wall coefficient; the scalar tutorials still use the first-order schemes.
\item Cost: one least-squares gradient per corrected field and component per assembly, the
      centroids, one coefficient and one offset per face, and one tensor per wall cell. The utility \code{ibmOrderTest} solves the
      manufactured problems of Section~\ref{sec:verifsecond} with the library operators.
\end{itemize}
\section{Implementation in OpenFOAM}
\label{sec:impl}

\subsection{Explicit operators on an unmodified mesh}

The mesh is left untouched; $\theta_f$ and $\alpha_P$ enter the standard operators
explicitly. The momentum equation of the transient solver reads
\begin{lstlisting}
fvm::ddt(ibm.alpha(), U)                      // alpha V/dt; alpha.oldTime() = alpha^n
+ fvm::div(phi, U)                            // phi = theta (U_f . Sf) = ibm.flux(U)
- fvm::laplacian(ibm.thetaInterpolate(nu), U) // theta-weighted, no wall term
+ fvm::Sp(nu*ibm.noSlipCoeff(), U)            // wall traction, no-slip bodies
+ fvm::Sp(nu*ibm.blankCoeff(), U)             // blanking of solid cells
== fvOptions(U) - ibm.grad(p)                 // sum_f theta_f (p_f - p_P) Sf / V
\end{lstlisting}
and, for moving bodies, \code{UEqn -= ibm.movingWallSource(nu)} adds
$\nu(\cns+b)\ub$.
The pressure equation is
\begin{lstlisting}
fvm::laplacian(ibm.thetaInterpolate(rAtU), p) == fvc::div(phiHbyA)  // phiHbyA = ibm.flux(HbyA)
pEqn -= ibm.massSource();          // moving bodies only: div(phi) = -S
ibm.constrainPressure(pEqn);       // regular rows for the solid cells
\end{lstlisting}
and \code{pEqn.flux()} is then automatically $\theta$-weighted. Everything else is
\code{simpleFoam} and \code{pimpleFoam}: the boundary constraints on $\bm H/a_P$ and $p$, the
pressure reference, pressure relaxation, finite-volume options, residual control and the
PIMPLE loops. The steady and transient solvers differ from their stock counterparts only in
the lines above, the floors of Section~\ref{sec:simplec} and the forces%
; the moving-body operations sit behind \code{if (ibm.moving())} guards in the
transient solver (geometry update and flux re-interpolation, moving-wall source,
mass source, continuity report), and the steady solver rejects moving bodies%
.

This choice keeps the full cell volume $V_P$ in \code{fvMatrix::A()}, so that
$r_A=V_P/a_P$ is exactly the mobility of \eqref{eq:rA}. Scaling the face area vectors and
cell volumes of the mesh in place would make all operators $\theta$/$\alpha$-aware
implicitly, but would change the SIMPLEC mobility to $\alpha V_P/a_P$, break the interpolation
weights on closed faces and require floors for solid cells; explicit operators are transparent
and term-for-term identical to the formulation above.
The two-level unsteady term of Table~\ref{tab:moving} costs no solver code: the density-weighted
time derivative \code{fvm::ddt(rho, U)} of OpenFOAM puts $\rho^{n+1}V/\dt$ on the diagonal and
uses the old-time value of the weight in its source, so the swept fraction $\alpha^n$ of
\eqref{eq:candB} is simply stored as the old-time value of $\alpha$ after the geometry update.

\section{Verification}
\label{sec:verif}

Unless stated otherwise the cases use a circular cylinder of radius $R=1$ centred in a
$10\times10$ doubly periodic box discretised by $81\times81$ cells ($h=0.1235$), $\nu=1$,
$\alpha_{\min}=0.02$, $n_s=32$ and central differencing for convection. The reference is an
independent implementation of the same discretisation on a structured grid (direct sparse
solves, exact pinning of the bulk velocity by superposition), so that the comparison isolates
the OpenFOAM implementation from the formulation. It differs from the OpenFOAM implementation
in details that do not change the discrete fixed point: it uses the level set $r^2-R^2$, a
penalty of $10^{12}$ instead of the blanking diagonal, floors $a_P$ instead of
$A-H1$, and pins the bulk velocity exactly instead of by the controller of
Section~\ref{sec:meanforce}. Forces are quoted per unit depth and density.

\subsection{Body at rest}

The flow is driven at bulk velocity $0.5$ ($Re_D=1$). Table~\ref{tab:static_verif} compares
geometry, fields and drag. The fractions agree to $10^{-11}$ and the cell fractions exactly.
The transient solver run with the reference's pseudo-time step ($\dt=65h^2/\nu$, Euler, one
corrector, SIMPLEC, no \code{ddtCorr}) converges to the same discrete fixed point (velocity to
$10^{-5}$, drag to $2\times10^{-6}$); the steady solver differs through the relaxation
dependence of the Rhie--Chow dissipation, the pressure differences being concentrated in the
cut cells. The body given as an STL (a 3600-gon) or as a fraction field gives the accuracy of
its geometry route. With two bodies of mixed wall conditions (a no-slip cylinder of radius $1$
and a free-slip one of radius $0.7$) the forces sum to the driving force times the fluid volume
to all printed digits, $0.625915+0.202512=0.828427$, the check of
Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation}; the free-slip body carries no viscous force. A four-rank
parallel run reproduces the serial drag to all printed digits.

\subsection{Moving body: free stream, oscillation, Galilean invariance}
\label{sec:galilean}

The moving cases use $\dt=0.025$, three outer iterations, one corrector, SIMPLEC and no
transient Rhie--Chow correction (\code{ddtCorr no}), as the reference.

\emph{Free-stream preservation.} The domain, solid included, is filled with $\uu=\ub$ and the
body translates at $\ub$ for five steps, with the forcing targeting $\ub$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:freestream}). The maximum departure from $\ub$ over the fluid is $0.0$
in $u$ and below $2\times10^{-15}$ in $v$ for $u_b=0.37$ and $u_b=2.0$ and for one or three
outer iterations; the reference gives $5.6\times10^{-16}$ and $3.6\times10^{-15}$. The test
exercises the two-level unsteady term, the mass source, the moving-wall traction, the blanking
and the flux re-interpolation simultaneously; a sign error in any of them gives an $O(1)$
result.

\emph{Oscillating cylinder.} $x_c=5+\sin(2\pi\cdot0.05\,t)$, $t\in[0,10]$, 400 steps, bulk
velocity held at zero. The force histories agree to $4.4\times10^{-4}$ after the impulsive
first ten steps ($7\times10^{-5}$ of the peak force; the first step, $F=-60.6$, agrees to
$0.2\%$), the driving force to $2.6\times10^{-6}$, and the final fields to $2\times10^{-7}$ in
velocity (Fig.~\ref{fig:osc}). The domain sum of the mass source is below $5\times10^{-18}$
and the continuity error below $2\times10^{-16}$ at every step (Fig.~\ref{fig:movingdiag}); in
25 of the 400 steps one or two cells at the rear of the body have $\alpha^n_P<0$, without
consequence. A four-rank run reproduces the serial forces to $2\times10^{-12}$.

\emph{Galilean invariance.} A body translating at $u_b=0.5$ through a fluid of zero bulk
velocity is the Galilean image of the body at rest in a bulk flow of $0.5$, but is computed
through entirely different terms. Over $t\in[0,30]$ the body crosses the periodic seam twice.
The mean force over the last 400 steps is $-5.69099$ with standard deviation $0.0883$, against
$-5.69103\pm0.0884$ for the reference; the steady solver gives $+5.66038$ for the body at
rest. The standard deviation ($1.5\%$) is the body sliding from one cut pattern to the next,
the characteristic signature of a cut-cell method on a non body-fitted mesh; the $0.5\%$
difference between the moving and the fixed body is not analysed further here (the swept-volume
integration is first order in time, and the moving body averages the first-order wall error
over all cut patterns while the fixed one sees a single pattern). The histories agree with the
reference to $1.3\times10^{-3}$ at every step after the impulsive start (Fig.~\ref{fig:gal}).

This test exposed a subtlety of the seam treatment. The reference, as first written, copied
$\theta$ across the periodic seam \emph{before} absorbing slivers, so an absorbed cell at
$x=L$ closed its face on that side while the same face stayed open for the cell at $x=0$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sliver}c); while the body front or back crosses the seam its force deviates by
up to $2.7\%$ for a few steps. Synchronising the coupled faces with the minimum \emph{after}
absorption (Section~\ref{sec:absorb}) keeps closure exact, the mass-source sum being
$10^{-19}$ throughout; applying the same rule to the reference removes the deviations, and the
numbers above are against that reference.

\subsection{Rotating bodies}
\label{sec:verifmoving}

\begin{sloppypar}
Three tutorial cases check the rotational source \eqref{eq:srot}: a cylinder turning in place
(\code{rotatingCylinder}), the same cylinder turning about a point off its axis
(\code{eccentricRotation}) and Taylor--Couette flow
(\code{order/taylorCouette}).
At every step the solver logs the domain sum of the source, its largest value and the largest
change of the fluid fraction, $\max|\alpha^{n+1}_P-\alpha^n_P|$. The three translating cases above
are bit-identical before and after the change, in every field of every write time and in the
force histories.
\end{sloppypar}

\emph{Cylinder turning in place} ($R=1$, $\omega=1$, centre off the mesh lines, $81^2$ cells,
$h=0.123$). With the earlier point evaluation the source was non-zero in all 63 wall cells
(largest $|S_P|=1.2\times10^{-3}$ per cell depth) and its domain sum was $-1.9\times10^{-4}$
instead of zero, the latter from a single nearly full cell whose evaluation point
$\xx_P+\dw\nn_w$ lay five units away and was wrapped by the minimum image. With \eqref{eq:srot}
the source vanishes to round-off in 58 of the 63 wall cells; the 5 cells next to removed cells
carry $|S_P|\le1.3\times10^{-3}$ and $|\alpha^{n+1}_P-\alpha^n_P|\le0.018$, the flux through
their closed faces (Proposition~\ref{prop:srot}); the domain sum is $10^{-18}$. That nearly full
cell now also receives the right wall velocity for its traction, $|\ub|=1.01$ instead of $4.5$,
the evaluation point being limited to the cell in 16 wall cells (Section~\ref{sec:ubpoint}).

\emph{Eccentric rotation.} The same cylinder turning about a point $(0.3,-0.2)$ off its axis
(motion entry \code{centreOfRotation}) sweeps, uncovers and absorbs cells every step. The domain
sum of the source stays below $10^{-16}$ at every step for sources up to
$9\times10^{-3}$; a four-rank run, with processor boundaries through the body, reproduces the
serial largest source at every step.

\emph{Taylor--Couette flow}: Section~\ref{sec:tc}.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{Order.} First order in time (implicit Euler%
      , inherited by the swept-volume integration%
      ).
      First order in space for no-slip walls with the treatment of
      Section~\ref{sec:static}, second order for the operators and the bulk velocity with that
      of Section~\ref{sec:secondorder}; cut-cell pressure and forces first order in both.
      A second-order extension in time would need $\alpha$ at three levels and a
      three-level form of \eqref{eq:candB}.
\item \emph{Cut-cell pressure.} The mobility of a cut cell scales with its fluid fraction, so
      its pressure is poorly determined%
      \ (Section~\ref{sec:smallcellp})%
      ; the wall pressure is taken as the cell pressure%
      \ (integrated over the wall pieces with the least-squares gradient under the
      second-order treatment, exact for a linear pressure, Section~\ref{sec:grad2};
      the pressure of the cut cells still does not converge)%
      . Cell merging or a wall pressure from the full cells is the remedy.
\item \emph{Slivers.} Absorption below $\alpha_{\min}$ displaces the boundary by
      $O(\alpha_{\min}h)$ and removes the fluid volume of the absorbed cell%
      ; a cell crossing the threshold hands over no momentum%
      \ or scalar content%
      . Mass conservation of the flow is unaffected. Cell merging is the clean remedy%
      \ and would also relax the step restriction%
      . A cut cell whose faces all close (thin features, or after coupled-face
      synchronisation) while its $\alpha$ stays above $\alpha_{\min}$ is not guarded against.
      $\alpha_{\min}$ must be positive, otherwise cells with $\alpha=0$ from a fraction field keep
      open faces.
\item \emph{Sub-cell features.} Faces are classified by their vertices and cells by their
      vertices, face centres and centre; a feature that passes between them is not seen.
\item \emph{Laminar.} Turbulence models are constructed for their effective viscosity only;
      their own transport equations would need the same treatment as%
      \ the scalars.
\item \emph{Fraction-field bodies.} Reconstructing $\theta$ from the $0.5$ iso-surface of the
      point-interpolated fraction smears the wall over one cell (drag $-0.16\%$ on the
      cylinder, $\theta$ errors up to $0.11$); a per-cell iso-value reproducing each cell's
      $\alpha$ would bring it to $O(h^2)$.
      These bodies have no centroids and stay first order.
\item \emph{One fluid region.} A single pressure reference; a body splitting the fluid would
      need one per region.
\item \emph{Rotating bodies.} The mass source is the exact flux of the rigid motion through
      the wall of the post-processed cell, compatible for every rigid motion
      (Section~\ref{sec:ubpoint}). A face closed by sliver absorption belongs to that wall and
      moves with the body, so a body of revolution turning in place still has a source, and
      $\alpha^n\neq\alpha^{n+1}$, in the cells next to removed ones; a uniform field is
      nevertheless preserved and the domain sum is zero. The source is not compatible on faces
      between cells of two different rotating bodies. In three dimensions the wall of a cut
      cell is the polyhedral approximation of the surface, so the source of a sphere turning
      about a diameter is not exactly zero but converges with the geometry. The wall velocity
      of the no-slip traction is a point value, at \eqref{eq:ubpoint} limited to the cell.
\item \emph{Cost of moving bodies.} The level set is evaluated at all points, face and cell
      centres every step, and $n_s^d$ samples per cut cell; for three-dimensional
      triangulated bodies a smaller $n_s$ or a band around the previous position is advisable.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions}

The method rests on one geometric statement, the closure identity of a cut cell%
, and on one choice for moving bodies, the wall flux as the primary quantity%
. From the closure identity follow exactness for uniform flow, the absence of spurious pressure
forces, no-penetration itself, discrete conservation of momentum and exact compatibility of
the pressure system%
; from the flux-first choice follows a discrete geometric conservation law that
preserves a free stream to machine precision%
. The whole boundary treatment adds to the interior scheme two scalar fields, $\theta_f$ and
$\alpha_P$, one diagonal coefficient for a no-slip%
\ or fixed-value%
\ wall%
, and one source term for a moving wall%
.
Moving the cell values to the fluid centroids, with a centroid-consistent diffusive flux and
the centroid wall distance, makes the operators and the velocity second order without ghost
values or stencil growth, and integrating the wall pressure over the pieces of the geometric
wall makes the pressure gradient exact for a linear pressure; the pressure of the cut cells,
and with it the forces, remains the open problem.
Implemented in OpenFOAM with explicit operators on an unmodified mesh, it reproduces an
independent reference implementation to the level of the linear-solver tolerances for a body
at rest%
, oscillating and translating through a periodic boundary%
, and transports passive scalars with budgets that close to round-off%
.
